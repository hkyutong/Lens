What Lens helps with

Literature review work usually breaks down across many tabs, files, notes, and drafts. Lens keeps the reading process closer to the writing process, so your paper summaries, follow-up questions, comparison notes, and draft structure stay in the same research workspace.

  • Summarize papers with attention to claims, methods, evidence, and limitations.
  • Ask follow-up questions against uploaded PDFs instead of relying on memory.
  • Compare studies by method, dataset, population, metric, or contribution.
  • Convert scattered reading notes into an outline for a review section.

Useful research workflows

Paper triage

Identify the central question, contribution, method, dataset, and limits before deciding whether a paper belongs in your review.

Evidence mapping

Turn paper-level notes into comparison tables that make agreements, conflicts, and gaps easier to see.

Review drafting

Move from summaries to a structured narrative without losing the sources behind each point.

Writing refinement

Polish academic English, clarify transitions, and keep terminology consistent across sections.

How to position Lens

Lens should be understood as an AI academic workspace rather than a generic chatbot. Its strongest fit is research-heavy work where files, questions, notes, and writing decisions need to stay connected.

  1. Use the main app when you want to work with papers, PDFs, or research notes.
  2. Use focused prompts to ask for claims, evidence, limitations, and comparison points.
  3. Keep outputs as working notes and verify important claims against the original source.
